Getting found off the website

The site is done. Two plans are live, the fee is stated one way everywhere, llms.txt and llms-full.txt are serving, and every page carries structured data. That work made Frontier quotable. This is the other half: making Frontier findable. None of it lives in the repo, and most of it is worth more than another landing page.

The premise

Why this beats writing more pages

When someone asks ChatGPT or Claude “who manages cabins in Broken Bow,” the model is not ranking websites the way Google does. It retrieves whatever sources discuss the topic, then synthesises an answer. In practice it leans on:

  • Google Business Profiles and the text inside their reviews
  • Third-party listicles — the “best property managers in Broken Bow” posts
  • Reddit, forums, and Facebook groups where real owners name names
  • Directory listings that confirm the business exists and where

Your own site is one voice in that set, and it is the one every model discounts, because every company says good things about itself. A single review that reads “Hunter drove out at 9pm when our hot tub quit” does more for AI-driven discovery than three more pages of copy.

The site is what converts someone once they arrive. Everything below is what gets Frontier into the answer in the first place.

Priority 1

Google Business Profile

The highest-leverage asset you own, and the one most small operators leave half-finished.

Set the Place ID before anything else

Two features already built into the site are sitting dormant waiting on this one value.

  1. Find the Place ID with Google’s Place ID finder
  2. Add it to the Vercel environment: NEXT_PUBLIC_GOOGLE_PLACE_ID=ChIJ…
  3. Redeploy
What that unlocks

The site’s sameAs declaration switches from a share.google redirect to the canonical Maps URL, which is a materially stronger entity signal. And rentwithfrontier.com/review becomes a one-tap deep link straight into the review dialog instead of dropping people on the profile to hunt for the star widget.

Categories are the strongest single ranking factor on a profile, and launching the Local Services plan makes Frontier legitimately eligible for a set it could not claim before.

“Broken Bow cabin cleaning service” is a real search with real volume and almost no competition from companies that also do management. Only ever claim a category you genuinely serve — but you do serve these now, and leaving them off keeps the new plan invisible.

Finish the profile

  • Description. Lead with the two plans and the market. Reuse the summary at the top of llms.txt; it was written for exactly this job.
  • Services. Enter every item from the Local Services page as its own service entry. Free keyword surface, and almost nobody fills it in.
  • Photos. Twenty minimum, weighted toward work rather than interiors — a turnover in progress, a finished cabin, the truck, you on site. Interiors read as a rental listing; work photos read as a service business. Add a few every month, because recency is scored.
  • Q&A. Seed it yourself. Post the five questions owners always ask and answer them from the business account. This section is a documented AI-retrieval source and it sits empty on nearly every competitor profile in the market.
  • Hours. Must match the site exactly — Mon–Fri, 9–5. Mismatches suppress ranking.

Posts

Weekly, and nearly free now that the pipeline exists: the captions at /admin/social paste into a Business Profile post exactly as well as they paste into Facebook. Same text, third destination.

Priority 2

Reviews

Volume, recency, and text quality, in that order. Ten reviews from the last six months beat forty from three years ago.

Who to askWhenWhy it matters
Guests of managed cabinsCheckout dayHighest volume. Automate it into the message flow.
Owner clientsAfter a save — a fixed disaster, a strong monthLow volume, highest value. This is what a prospective owner actually reads.
Local Services clientsAfter the first monthA source that did not exist before this plan launched.
Vendors and contractorsAnytimeLegitimate reciprocal relationships.

How to ask

Text them rentwithfrontier.com/review. That is the entire ask. Do not send a paragraph explaining why reviews matter to small businesses — conversion drops with every additional sentence.

Do not

Never offer anything in exchange for a review. Google filters incentivised reviews and can penalise the profile that solicited them.

Responding

Reply to every review within 48 hours, and write the reply for the next reader rather than the reviewer. Work the market and the service in naturally:

Thanks Sarah — glad the freeze check gave you peace of mind. That January cold snap caught a lot of Hochatown cabins off guard.

That reply is now indexable text on your profile containing “freeze check,” “Hochatown,” and “January.” Multiply it by fifty reviews and the profile starts describing your service better than your homepage does.

On negative reviews: answer factually, never defensively, and never with detail that identifies the guest. A calm reply to a bad review persuades more readers than five five-star ratings.

Priority 4

The mentions that actually get cited

The hardest section here, and the one with the highest ceiling.

Get into the listicles

Search “best property management companies Broken Bow” and “Broken Bow Airbnb management.” Whoever ranks for those is being fed to language models as the answer. Most are affiliate roundups that will add you if you ask. Email the author with the two plans, the fee structure, and the boutique angle. It is unglamorous outreach and it works.

Be genuinely useful in public

r/AirBnB, r/airbnb_hosts, r/OklahomaCity, and the Broken Bow owner Facebook groups all get scraped. Answer short-term rental questions as an operator, without pitching, occasionally, signed with who you are. Models cite Reddit heavily, and a manager who explains occupancy tax filing clearly in a thread becomes a named entity in the model’s picture of this market.

Local press and podcasts

McCurtain Daily Gazette, Oklahoma tourism outlets, short-term rental podcasts. “Boutique manager who caps their portfolio and operates their own cabin” is an actual story angle, which is more than most operators can offer a journalist.

Guest posts

One well-placed post on an established industry site, linking back, is worth more than ten more articles on your own domain.

Priority 5

Facebook, on rails

The pipeline is built. Discipline is the only missing part.

  1. Publish the article to the site
  2. Open /admin/social?token=… on your phone
  3. Copy, paste to Facebook
  4. Paste the same text as a Google Business Profile post

One article, three surfaces, under five minutes. Twice a month is enough — consistency beats volume, and a page that posts twice a month for a year outperforms one that posts daily for six weeks and then goes silent.

Between articles: storm and freeze notices, a before-and-after turnover, a seasonal booking note. Local and useful, not promotional.

Instrumentation

What to measure

Monthly, not weekly. Local search moves slowly, and weekly checking produces noise and then bad decisions made on the noise.

The one that costs nothing

Add “ChatGPT or another AI assistant” as an explicit option on your discovery-call intake question. You already know owners are arriving that way, but right now you cannot prove how many — and without that baseline there is no way to tell whether any of this worked.
